Chapter 1: Ideology of Pakistan

CSS Focus Note: This topic is fundamental for paper 1. Focus on the historical evolution, Two-Nation Theory, and contributions of key figures.

1. Definition and Meaning of Ideology

An ideology represents a collection of systematic beliefs, values, and ideas that shape the political, social, and cultural identity of a nation. The Ideology of Pakistan is based on Islamic Principles and the fundamental distinction between Muslims and Hindus in South Asia.

2. Two-Nation Theory (Do-Qoumi Nazriya)

The Two-Nation Theory states that Muslims and Hindus are two separate nations in South Asia based on different religious traditions, social customs, literature, and legal norms.

  • Religious Basis: Islam is a complete code of life, distinct from Hinduism.
  • Cultural Identity: Separate food habits, language (Urdu vs Hindi), and social values.
  • Political Representation: Demand for separate electorates to prevent majority domination.

3. Role of Key Personalities

Sir Syed Ahmad Khan (1817–1898)

First leader to use the term 'Nation' for Muslims after the 1857 war. Founded Aligarh Movement for modern education and requested separate political safeguards.

Allama Muhammad Iqbal (1877–1938)

Presented the idea of a separate Muslim state in the famous Allahabad Address of 1930, focusing on northwestern Muslim-majority areas.

Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah (1876–1948)

Transformed the ideological concept into a practical state through the Lahore Resolution of 1940 and negotiated constitutional guarantees with the British.
